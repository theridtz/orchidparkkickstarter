Delivery key

How to read the cues

Read SAY text aloud where useful and adapt the words while keeping the instruction. Give one instruction at a time, demonstrate the task, then ask a student to explain the next action before starting the timer.

Reference · Scene 06 · slides 50–65

Creator profiles

Spend 30–35 seconds per type: name it, describe the strength, show the example on the adjacent slide. Preview any clip first. Do not infer empathy, intelligence or personality levels from the quiz. A quiet student can write a strong Yapper script; an Edutainer can use humour.

Reference · slides 66–80

Six frames, five beats

The extra frame, Set the Scene, establishes who and where while already showing something interesting. Six frames are planning beats, not a rule for exactly six camera shots. Switch timings and tap a frame.

Worked example · Creating an Inclusive Community

Audience: OPSS students. Format: mini skit. Key message: a small, specific invitation can help someone join in. Techniques: close-ups, captions and an emotional shift. Fictional characters, consenting cast. The context frame already contains the attention trigger; the insight is a new understanding and the solution is visible behaviour. Read the words aloud and time them before filming.

Reference · Scene 05 · slides 43–48

The seven-point split

Two cards, seven points, positive whole numbers only. Pick a split to see what gets written on each card this round. Possible totals after five rounds: 5–30. Ties are allowed.

Card A

“Worried you won't fit in?” · a student hesitates outside a classroom; a classmate makes space.

5
Round 1 = 5
Card B

“Welcome to OPSS!” · wide shot of the school gate.

2
Round 1 = 2

Front of the card

Name or student code · Audience: P6 students considering OPSS · Hook line or caption · Opening scene

Back of the card

Rounds 1 to 5 with one score each, then a Total. Example: 4 + 6 + 3 + 5 + 4 = 22.

Rules on screen

Compare. Split seven. Record one score per card. Exchange cards, find a new partner. Exactly five rounds. Never score your own work.

Reel rubric /20

Audience voting selects the Best Reel; the rubric explains creative choices and breaks ties. It does not predict online reach. Any unresolved consent or privacy issue must be fixed before screening, whatever the other scores.
